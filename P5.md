# P5.md — the kea as a real asset: plan, sourcing, and the three decisions that are Eric's

Companion to REPLAT.md (which governs) and WAVES.md. Written 2026-09-04, session 23, **before any
change to the rig** — that ordering was the brief's, and it turned out to matter: what the survey
found changes what P5 is.

---

## 0. WHAT P5 ACTUALLY IS, AND IT IS NOT WHAT THE CLAUSE SAYS

REPLAT.md P5 says *"retarget the existing animations to its skeleton."* That sentence assumes the
bird has **animation clips**. It does not. I read the whole `Kea` class before sourcing anything:

> **There are no clips. There is a hand-written procedural rig with 80 pose-write sites.**

Every frame, `Kea.update()` writes rotations directly onto named `THREE.Group`s:

| handle | write sites | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `wings[0..1]` | 27 | flap, fold, mantle, the stall-flare |
| `head` | 19 | look, cock, the preen tuck |
| `body` | 17 | lean, the luge posture, hop compression |
| `neck` | 16 | stretch (it **scales**, not just rotates), turn |
| `tailF[0..4]` | 11 | the five tail feathers **fan individually** |
| `jaw` | 9 | bite, scream, the seal-working chew |
| `tail` | 8 | up/down |
| `legs[0..1]` | 3 | walk cycle, the luge tuck |
| `beakTip` | — | read-only world anchor: **what missions attach to** |

So P5 is not retargeting. **It is re-binding a procedural rig onto a skeleton**, and the model's
*bone structure* matters far more than its looks. The minimum a candidate skeleton must provide:

    body · neck · head · jaw · wing ×2 · tail root · tail feather ×5 · leg ×2   = 13 joints
    plus a stable point at the beak tip for the mission anchor

A model with a beautiful mesh and eight anonymous bones costs more to adopt than a plain one with
thirteen named ones. That is the axis I sourced on, and it is the axis I would ask you to choose on.

### The finding that is not on any product page

I opened D and E's `.blend` files directly (the data region before the `DNA1` block carries the
real names) and read their armatures out:

    D (CC0)    Bone, Bone.001 … Bone.020     ~21 bones, EVERY ONE UNNAMED
    E (CC-BY)  Bone, Bone.001 … Bone.008      ~9 bones, EVERY ONE UNNAMED

**Both rigs are anonymous.** With bones called `Bone.014`, the map from `this.wings[0]` to a bone
cannot be written from the file — it has to be established by eye in a 3D viewer and then hard-coded
as an index, which is exactly the kind of constant a battery cannot check and a later session
cannot re-derive. That is a real, recurring cost, and it is invisible until you open the file.

## 2. THE PLAN, ONCE A MODEL IS CHOSEN

Sequenced so the tree is never broken and each step is provable on its own.

1. **P5a — the asset lands and is proved.** Convert to `.glb`, decimate to budget, record the
   licence line *and* the publisher md5 in `assets/LICENCES.md` before first use. Battery: the file
   loads headless, its skin and bone count are asserted, licence ledger entry exists and its md5
   matches. **No game change yet.**
2. **P5b — the joint map, as a named table.** One constant block mapping each of the 13 procedural
   handles to a bone, with a battery that resolves every entry against the loaded skeleton and goes
   red on a missing or duplicate binding. This is where the anonymous-bone cost is paid, once, in a
   place that is readable.
3. **P5c — the bird is swapped behind the existing interface.** `Kea.buildMesh()` builds from the
   GLB; `this.head/neck/jaw/wings/tailF/legs/beakTip` become bone references. **Every one of the 80
   write sites keeps working unchanged** — that is the test of whether the map is right.
   PROOF: all mission anchors resolve, **seal mission 12/12 headless**, nine batteries green.
4. **P5d — the kea, judged.** Olive plumage, scarlet underwing coverts with barring, slate hooked
   bill, grey zygodactyl feet, against `kea_head_01/02`, `kea_posture_01/02`, `kea_underwing_01`,
   `kea_feet_01`, `kea_scale_01`. Variant strip, per the WAVES law of best. **Your call, not mine.**
5. **P5e — the readability re-tune.** The subject classifiers are calibrated on the *current* bird's
   olive (`h52-80 s0.30-0.62`). A new mesh will move those numbers and `subjects.mjs` floors will
   need regrounding — the same discipline as TODO 81, measured against a bird-absent control.

**Risk I want on the record now:** step 5 means the new bird will move `03_kea_plate`,
`13_idle_preen`, `08_readability_320` and the boxdiff subjects. That is expected, it is not a
regression, and the floors must be regrounded against a control rather than fitted to the frame.

## 3. THE THREE DECISIONS THAT ARE YOURS

**(1) Which base model — or none of them.** My honest read: **A is the only one that gets us toward
the Birds of War target**, and it costs a decimation pass and a silhouette that is a macaw rather
than a kea. B, C, D and E are all *sideways* moves from the procedural bird — better topology, not
better looking. "None of these, widen the search or pay for one" is a legitimate answer and I have
not ruled it out. I have deliberately not picked.

**(2) Blender is not installed.** The brief said it was available; it is not on this machine.
`brew` is, and `blender` is a cask at 5.2.1. Every route from here needs it — `.blend` → `.glb`,
decimation, the recolour, and reading an anonymous rig well enough to map it. Shall I run
`brew install --cask blender`? It is a ~1GB install on your machine, so I have not done it.

**(3) Sketchfab downloads need an account.** A, B and C are all Sketchfab. Their metadata API is
public (that is where the table above comes from) but `/download` returns
`"Authentication credentials were not provided."` D and E came from OpenGameArt and downloaded fine.
Either you download the chosen one and drop it in `assets/`, or you give me another route.

## 7. DECIDED — F, and what landed 2026-09-04 (P5a)

**Eric chose F, the black palm cockatoo.** Disk cleared, Blender install retried, and he takes the
Sketchfab download himself. What shipped in this piece is everything that can be done *before* the
bytes arrive — deliberately, so the asset lands into a tree that is already ready to receive it.

**Blender 5.2.1 LTS is installed** and verified headless with glTF import (`bpy` 5.2.1, importer
present). The first attempt died on `No space left on device` at 99% full; after Eric cleared space
it went through clean.

### The obligation that arrived with the licence

This is **the project's first CC-BY asset**. Everything before it was CC0 from Poly Haven, where
attribution is a courtesy the ledger paid anyway. Sketchfab's licence field for the cockatoo reads:

> **"Author must be credited. Commercial use is allowed."**

That is a *condition of use*, and it has a consequence nobody had to think about until now: **the
game had no credits surface at all.** A credit that lives only in `assets/LICENCES.md` is not a
credit to a player who has the game and not the repo. So:

- `CREDITS` in `src/game.mjs` — one source of truth, listing the CC-BY cockatoo **and** the CC0
  Poly Haven authors, who are named precisely because nothing compels it and they would therefore
  be the first to be dropped in a hurry.
- Rendered onto the **title screen** at boot, before anything is played — not behind a menu a
  player may never open. Built from text nodes, never `innerHTML`.
- `assets/LICENCES.md` carries a machine-readable `<!-- ASSET ... -->` marker per asset from P5
  onward, and **a battery cross-checks the ledger against `CREDITS` in both directions**: add an
  asset without a credit and the gate is red; delete a credit while the ledger still lists the
  asset and the gate is red. Neither is catchable by reading.
- **A landed file cannot sit on a `PENDING` md5.** The rule is: either the file exists and its md5
  matches, or the file does not exist and the md5 says PENDING. Proved by sabotage in both
  directions.

Twelve sabotages against this chain, **all twelve red**.
